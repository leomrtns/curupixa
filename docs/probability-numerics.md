# Probability kernels and provenance
The public `prob_distribution.h` interface retains its existing signatures: gamma takes shape and rate, normal takes mean and standard deviation, and `log_p` selects log probabilities or log probability inputs for quantiles. The R-derived numerical kernels have been replaced. The library links platform libm, including its re-entrant `lgamma_r`; it has no R, GSL, SciPy or C++ runtime dependency.
## Source provenance
| Files | Current source and method |
| --- | --- |
| `probability_special_internal.c`, `probability_coefficients_internal.h` | C17 adaptation of the inverse-normal rational approximation and Temme gamma expansion from SciPy/Xsf commit `31177fd9a458539cf05c04d215341245d0245e53`. The coefficient tables and adapted calculations are BSD-3-Clause; SciPy and Stephen L. Moshier notices are retained in `LICENSES/`. Gamma series, continued fractions, log-tail evaluation and bracketed inversion follow the mathematical definitions below. |
| `prob_distribution.c`, `probability_sampling.c` | Public compatibility functions and libm operations, with random-state consumers in a separate object so scalar-only static clients do not pull in RNG implementations. Previously added Curupixa alias sampling and gamma categories are retained. Normal sampling now uses the polar method; Poisson sampling uses product inversion below mean 10 and Hoermann PTRS above it. Gamma sampling retains the Marsaglia–Tsang method. |
| `probability_extra.c` | New closed-form exponential, Weibull and logistic calculations, plus chi-square wrappers over gamma. These implementations were written for Curupixa. |
| `index_arrangement.c` | Newly written lexicographic permutation/combination traversal and composition. Permutation validation uses a bit set for up to 64 elements and a membership array for larger inputs; combination validation is linear. The public types and function parameters remain available. |
| `quasi_random.c`, `quasi_sequence.c` | The context-based interface now calls the independently implemented checked sequence module. Prime bases are generated there. The historical GSL/Trolltech constants file, including its disabled copied block, has been removed. |
SciPy/Xsf's own BSD licence and its separately documented Cephes BSD grant are included in source distributions and installed notices. No third-party GPL implementation was imported for these replacements. New Curupixa code retains the project's current GPL notice; this work does not change the repository's overall licence. The replacements address the R/GSL/qrng items in the [earlier provenance audit](261107.mit-licence-audit.md). The [RNG/hash cleanup](random-hashing.md) addresses further entries; other provenance and source-distribution findings still need review before a permissive release; this is not a declaration that the whole repository is cleared.
The mathematical references are [DLMF gamma series and fractions](https://dlmf.nist.gov/8.25), [uniform asymptotics](https://dlmf.nist.gov/8.12), [normal error functions](https://dlmf.nist.gov/7.2), and [Marsaglia and Tsang (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1145/358407.358414). Hoermann's PTRS is described in *The transformed rejection method for generating Poisson random variables*, Insurance: Mathematics and Economics 12 (1993), 39–45. The methods' dates alone do not determine their numerical quality or speed.
## Numerical behaviour
Gamma CDFs use direct lower or upper calculations and a uniform expansion around the large-shape transition. Quantiles use a bracketed log-tail solver with safeguarded higher-order steps. Normal CDFs use libm erfc, with a log-tail expansion after ordinary probabilities underflow. The inverse normal accepts log probabilities directly, including values far below the range of ordinary probabilities. `crpx_pgamma_tail()` and `crpx_pnorm_tail()` expose direct upper tails so callers need not subtract from a rounded value of one.
New exponential functions take a rate. Weibull functions take shape and scale. Logistic functions take location and scale; chi-square functions take degrees of freedom. All follow the existing d/p/q naming and `log_p` conventions. Their log calculations retain small tails and avoid unnecessary overflow. New tests include extreme tails, independently calculated fixtures and defining distribution identities.
Sampling sequences and consumption of random draws change for normal, gamma and Poisson draws. Reproducibility holds for identical streams and calls within this implementation, with no promise of reproducing the old generator outputs. The legacy Halton constructor now rejects dimensions outside 1..2048, matching the checked backend rather than reusing prime bases to invent additional coordinates. Original Halton retains its reversed coordinate order. Index structures should still be constructed through their constructors; the quasi-random object now owns backend objects too.
## Validation and timing
All existing regression suites run against the new implementation. Optional `tests/check_probability_reference.py` compares 5,000 gamma log CDF points and 1,000 gamma quantiles against SciPy, and checks extreme normal log tails with mpmath. The checked shape range for that comparison is `1e-5..1e5`; the worst observed scaled log-CDF error was `1.15e-14`. Larger-shape regression fixtures were calculated separately from high-precision defining series, rather than treating a reference library as infallible. In particular, the lower gamma log probability at shape `1e9`, argument `999000000` is approximately `-504.70739622249588484486`.
Local comparisons against commit `16a1862` showed substantial gains for Poisson generation: 20,000 draws took approximately 0.0012 versus 0.0066 seconds at mean 1, 0.0048 versus 0.0128 at mean 10, 0.0036 versus 0.0361 at mean 1,000, and 0.0033 versus 0.2257 at mean one million. Normal/gamma CDF performance improved or remained comparable in the mixed scalar cases tried. Some inverse-normal and inverse-gamma cases were slower, often by roughly 20–40%; timing varied between runs. The documented inverse-function trade-off was accepted for this revision. No blanket claim that every replacement is faster is made. Accuracy, permissive source provenance and measured costs are recorded separately so further tuning can be assessed against the same contracts.
